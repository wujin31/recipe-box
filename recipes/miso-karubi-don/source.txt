味噌カルビ丼 — Miso Karubi Don

Yakiniku-style miso-marinated short rib over rice. The miso-gochujang tare is the焼肉屋 (yakiniku-shop) flavor — savory, garlicky, a little sweet, a little spicy.

Ingredients
• 300 g thin-sliced beef short rib / beef belly (牛カルビ / 牛バラ)
• 1 1/2 tbsp miso (red/awase preferred)
• 1 tbsp gochujang
• 1 tbsp sugar
• 1 tbsp soy sauce
• 1 tbsp mirin
• 1 tbsp sake
• 1 tsp oyster sauce
• 1 tbsp sesame oil
• 1 garlic clove, grated
• 1 tsp ginger, grated
• 1 tbsp grated apple or onion (tenderizer, optional)
• 1 tbsp toasted white sesame seeds
• 2 warm cooked white rice (bowls)
• 2 scallions, thinly sliced
• 2 egg yolks (optional topping)

Steps
1. In a bowl, whisk together 1 1/2 tbsp miso (red/awase preferred), 1 tbsp gochujang, 1 tbsp sugar, 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp mirin, 1 tbsp sake, 1 tsp oyster sauce, 1 tbsp sesame oil, 1 garlic clove, grated, and 1 tsp ginger, grated until smooth. If using, stir in 1 tbsp grated apple or onion (tenderizer, optional) — the enzymes tenderize the beef.
2. Add 300 g thin-sliced beef short rib / beef belly (牛カルビ / 牛バラ) to the tare and massage it in well. Let it sit at least 30 minutes (or up to 2 hours in the fridge) so the flavor soaks in.
3. Heat a frying pan over high heat (no extra oil needed — the sesame oil and beef fat are enough). Lay 300 g thin-sliced beef short rib / beef belly (牛カルビ / 牛バラ) out in a single layer and sear, scraping in any tare left in the bowl. Cook just until caramelized and cooked through — don't overstir, you want browning. 5 minutes.
4. Mound 2 warm cooked white rice (bowls) into bowls. Pile the miso karubi on top and spoon over any pan juices. Scatter with 2 scallions, thinly sliced and 1 tbsp toasted white sesame seeds, and drop an 2 egg yolks (optional topping) in the center if you like. Break the yolk into the hot meat and mix as you eat.

Notes
Notes: Red or awase miso gives the deepest yakiniku flavor; if you only have white miso, add a little extra soy. For a hotter version bump the gochujang, or add a pinch of doubanjiang (豆板醤) as some shop recipes do. Sear over genuinely high heat and resist stirring — the browned, slightly charred edges are what make it taste like a restaurant. Good with namul, kimchi, or a simple lettuce/sanchu wrap on the side.
