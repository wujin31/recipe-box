Jollof Rice — Nigerian Party Jollof

Long-grain parboiled rice cooked in a deeply fried-down base of blended tomatoes, red peppers, onion and Scotch bonnet with curry powder, thyme and bay, finished on low heat so the bottom catches for the smoky "party" flavor. Serves 6.

Ingredients
For the pepper base:
• 4 plum tomatoes
• 2 red bell peppers, seeded
• 1 onion, roughly chopped
• 1–2 Scotch bonnet chiles, stemmed
For the rice:
• 120 ml vegetable oil
• 1 onion, thinly sliced
• 70 g tomato paste
• 2 tsp Nigerian-style curry powder
• 1 tsp dried thyme
• 3 bay leaves
• 2 bouillon cubes (Knorr or Maggi), crumbled
• 1 tsp salt, to taste
• 600 g long-grain parboiled rice
• 700 ml chicken stock
• 2 tbsp butter
• 2 tomatoes, sliced (to finish)
• 1 onion, sliced into rings (to finish)

Steps
1. Blend 4 plum tomatoes, 2 red bell peppers, seeded, 1 onion, roughly chopped and 1–2 Scotch bonnet chiles, stemmed until smooth. Simmer the purée in a pot for 15 minutes, until reduced by about a third.
2. Heat 120 ml vegetable oil in a heavy pot over medium. Fry 1 onion, thinly sliced for 3 minutes, add 70 g tomato paste and fry 5 minutes, stirring, until it darkens from red to brick.
3. Add the reduced pepper base, 2 tsp Nigerian-style curry powder, 1 tsp dried thyme, 3 bay leaves, 2 bouillon cubes (Knorr or Maggi), crumbled and 1 tsp salt, to taste. Fry 15 minutes, stirring often, until thick and the oil separates and floats on top. Don't rush this.
4. Rinse 600 g long-grain parboiled rice until the water runs clear and stir it into the base until every grain is coated. Pour in 700 ml chicken stock; the liquid should come just level with the rice.
5. Cover tightly with foil and then the lid. Cook on low for 30 minutes, without stirring, until the rice is tender and the liquid absorbed. If it's still firm, sprinkle with a few tablespoons of water, re-cover and cook 5 minutes more.
6. Raise the heat to medium for 3 minutes, without stirring, to let the bottom toast and smoke slightly.
7. Stir in 2 tbsp butter, lay 2 tomatoes, sliced (to finish) and 1 onion, sliced into rings (to finish) on top, cover for 5 minutes off the heat, then fluff and serve.

Notes
Parboiled (converted) long-grain rice is the Nigerian standard: it stays separate and won't turn mushy in the tomato base. Frying the base until the oil floats removes the raw, sour tomato taste.

The slightly scorched layer at the bottom is the prized "bottom pot" flavor of party jollof cooked over firewood. Serve with fried plantains, fried or grilled chicken, and coleslaw. Ghanaian jollof uses jasmine or basmati and often adds ginger and garlic.
