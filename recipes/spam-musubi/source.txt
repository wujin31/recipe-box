Spam Musubi — Hawaiian Spam Rice Block

Hawaii's convenience-store staple: Spam fried crisp and glazed in a sweet shoyu sauce, pressed onto a block of rice and wrapped with nori. Serves 8 (8 musubi).

Ingredients
• 1 can Spam (340 g), cut into 8 slices
• 3 tbsp shoyu (soy sauce)
• 3 tbsp sugar
• 2 tbsp mirin
• 900 g hot cooked short-grain rice
• 1 tbsp rice vinegar
• 2 tbsp furikake (optional)
• 4 sheets nori, cut in half crosswise

Steps
1. Stir 1 tbsp rice vinegar into 900 g hot cooked short-grain rice and keep it covered and warm.
2. Mix 3 tbsp shoyu (soy sauce), 3 tbsp sugar and 2 tbsp mirin.
3. Fry 1 can Spam (340 g), cut into 8 slices in a dry nonstick pan over medium-high for 2 minutes per side, until browned and crisp.
4. Turn the heat to low, pour in the sauce and turn the slices for 1 minute, until the sauce is syrupy and coats the Spam.
5. Lay a half sheet of 4 sheets nori, cut in half crosswise shiny side down with a musubi mold across its center. Press in about 110 g rice firmly, sprinkle with 2 tbsp furikake (optional), top with a Spam slice and press again.
6. Push the musubi out of the mold, fold the nori up and over, and seal the end with a grain of rice. Repeat with the rest.

Notes
No mold? Use the empty Spam can, both ends cut off and lined with plastic wrap. Wrapped tightly in plastic, musubi keep at room temperature for the day; don't refrigerate, as the rice hardens.

Popular add-ons: a slice of tamagoyaki between the rice and Spam, or a layer of rice on top too (double-decker).
