French Onion Grilled Cheese

French onion soup as a sandwich: onions caramelized slowly and deglazed with sherry and beef stock until jammy, layered with nutty Gruyère between buttery sourdough, griddled with a frico-crisp Parmesan crust. Serves 2.

Ingredients
• 2 large yellow onions (about 500 g), thinly sliced
• 2 tbsp butter (for the onions)
• 1/4 tsp salt
• 1/2 tsp fresh thyme leaves
• 60 ml dry sherry or dry white wine
• 120 ml beef stock
• 1/2 tsp Worcestershire sauce
• 1/4 tsp black pepper
• 4 slices sourdough bread, 1.5 cm thick
• 2 tbsp softened butter (for the bread)
• 150 g Gruyère, grated
• 20 g Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated

Steps
1. Melt 2 tbsp butter (for the onions) in a wide skillet over medium. Add 2 large yellow onions (about 500 g), thinly sliced and 1/4 tsp salt and cook 10 minutes, stirring, until soft. Lower the heat to medium-low and cook 35 minutes more, stirring every few minutes and scraping the browned bits, until deep amber and jammy. Add a splash of water if they start to catch.
2. Add 1/2 tsp fresh thyme leaves and 60 ml dry sherry or dry white wine and scrape the pan as it bubbles away, about 1 minute. Add 120 ml beef stock and 1/2 tsp Worcestershire sauce and simmer 4 minutes, until almost dry. Season with 1/4 tsp black pepper.
3. Spread 2 tbsp softened butter (for the bread) on one side of each slice of 4 slices sourdough bread, 1.5 cm thick and sprinkle 20 g Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated over the butter, pressing it in.
4. Flip two slices over and top each with a quarter of 150 g Gruyère, grated, half the onions and another quarter of the cheese. Close with the other slices, Parmesan side out.
5. Cook in a skillet over medium-low, covered, for 4 minutes per side, until the crust is deep golden and the cheese has melted. Rest 1 minute, then cut.

Notes
Caramelizing takes time: 45 minutes is not padding. The onions keep for a week in the fridge, so make a double batch. Warm extra beef stock with a splash of sherry for dipping, like a soup and sandwich in one.
