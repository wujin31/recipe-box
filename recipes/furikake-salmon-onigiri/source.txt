Furikake Salmon Onigiri — Hawaiian Furikake Salmon Rice Balls

The Hawaii potluck favorite, furikake salmon (baked under a mayo and furikake crust), flaked with Kewpie and tucked into rice balls rolled in more furikake. Serves 4 (8 onigiri).

Ingredients
• 350 g skinless salmon fillet
• 1/4 tsp salt
• 3 tbsp Kewpie mayonnaise
• 2 tsp shoyu (soy sauce)
• 1 tsp sugar
• 6 tbsp nori komi furikake
• 1 tbsp Kewpie mayonnaise (for the filling)
• 800 g hot cooked short-grain rice
• 1 tsp salt (for your hands)
• 2 sheets nori, each cut into 4 strips

Steps
1. Heat the oven to 200°C. Pat 350 g skinless salmon fillet dry and sprinkle with 1/4 tsp salt. Set it on a lined baking sheet.
2. Mix 3 tbsp Kewpie mayonnaise, 2 tsp shoyu (soy sauce) and 1 tsp sugar, spread it over the top of the salmon, and press 2 tbsp of 6 tbsp nori komi furikake over the mayo.
3. Bake 12 minutes, until the salmon is just cooked and the topping is browned. Cool 5 minutes, then flake it and fold in 1 tbsp Kewpie mayonnaise (for the filling).
4. Gently mix 2 tbsp of the remaining furikake into 800 g hot cooked short-grain rice.
5. Wet your hands and rub them with a pinch of 1 tsp salt (for your hands). Take about 100 g rice, add a spoonful of salmon in the center, close and press into a triangle.
6. Dip the edges in the remaining furikake and wrap with a strip of 2 sheets nori, each cut into 4 strips.

Notes
Nori komi furikake (sesame and seaweed) is the Hawaii standard; avoid the varieties with bonito if you want the classic flavor. The baked furikake salmon is also a dinner on its own: serve the whole fillet with rice. For musubi-style blocks, press the rice and salmon in a musubi mold instead.
