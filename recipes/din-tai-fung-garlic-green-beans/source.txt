Din Tai Fung Garlic Green Beans (蒜香四季豆)

Blistered, wrinkle-skinned green beans tossed with raw garlic, salt, and white pepper. The whole dish lives or dies on the oil-blister. UNTESTED — first cook to verify.

Ingredients
• 300 g green beans (four-season beans), trimmed
• 5 garlic, finely minced
• 4 tbsp neutral oil for shallow-frying
• 1/2 tsp fine salt
• 1/4 tsp white pepper
• 1/4 tsp chicken bouillon powder (optional, for the restaurant umami)

Steps
1. Trim 300 g green beans (four-season beans), trimmed and snap or cut into ~2-inch lengths. Pat BONE dry with a towel — any surface water will spit violently in hot oil. This is non-negotiable for both safety and blistering.
2. Add 4 tbsp neutral oil for shallow-frying to your stainless pan over medium-high. Heat until shimmering and a test bean sizzles aggressively on contact (~190°C / 375°F). On bare stainless, judge by the shimmer and a single test bean, not the IR gun — the polished surface reads low. 
3. Lay in 300 g green beans (four-season beans), trimmed in a single layer — do NOT crowd, or they steam instead of blister. Work in two batches if needed. Leave them mostly undisturbed, tossing only occasionally, until the skins wrinkle, pucker, and pick up brown-black spots and the beans go limp-tender. 6 minutes.
4. Pull the beans to a paper-towel-lined plate. Pour off all but about 1 tsp of oil from the pan (save it — it's bean-flavored).
5. Drop heat to medium-low so the pan cools slightly. Add 5 garlic, finely minced to the residual oil and stir for 15–20 seconds — just to take the raw bite off. Garlic burns instantly here, so move fast and don't let it brown past pale gold.
6. Return the beans, add 1/2 tsp fine salt, 1/4 tsp white pepper, and 1/4 tsp chicken bouillon powder (optional, for the restaurant umami) if using. Toss hard for 20–30 seconds to coat every bean in garlic. Taste, adjust salt, and plate immediately.

Notes
Stainless notes: the dry-bean rule matters double here — wet beans + 190°C oil = a spatter hazard on an open pan. Keep a splatter screen handy. The fond won't build much (it's an oil-fry, not a sear), so don't chase it.

Authenticity flags: DTF's is pure — resist adding soy sauce, sugar, or sesame oil, all of which are common Western "improvements" that push it toward a generic stir-fry. The garlic goes in RAW-ish and late; it should taste sharp and fresh, not deep-roasted. If you want the cold pork-mince Sichuan version (乾煸四季豆), that's a separate recipe entirely.

Air-fryer shortcut (if you ever want it): toss beans in 1 tbsp oil + salt, 180°C for ~10 min shaking halfway, then do the garlic toss in a pan. Lower fat, ~80% of the texture.

Open question for your cook: confirm whether the blister wants to go darker (more char spots) to match what you remember from the restaurant — DTF's tend to be fairly heavily spotted.
