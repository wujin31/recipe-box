Shoyu Poke Bowl — Hawaiian Ahi or Salmon Poke over Rice

Hawaii-style shoyu poke, not the build-your-own mainland kind: sashimi-grade ahi (or salmon) dressed with shoyu, sesame oil, sweet onion, limu and inamona, served over rice. Serves 3.

Ingredients
• 450 g sashimi-grade ahi tuna or salmon, cut into 2 cm cubes
• 3 tbsp shoyu (soy sauce)
• 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
• 1/4 sweet onion (Maui or Walla Walla), thinly slivered
• 2 scallions, sliced
• 15 g ogo (limu) seaweed, chopped (optional)
• 1 tsp inamona or 1 tbsp crushed toasted macadamia nuts
• 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds
• 1/4 tsp Hawaiian sea salt
• 1/4 tsp red chili flakes
• 600 g warm cooked short-grain rice
• 1 Persian cucumber, sliced
• 1 tbsp furikake

Steps
1. Pat 450 g sashimi-grade ahi tuna or salmon, cut into 2 cm cubes dry and put it in a chilled bowl.
2. Add 1/4 sweet onion (Maui or Walla Walla), thinly slivered, 2 scallions, sliced, 15 g ogo (limu) seaweed, chopped (optional), 1 tsp inamona or 1 tbsp crushed toasted macadamia nuts, 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds, 1/4 tsp Hawaiian sea salt and 1/4 tsp red chili flakes. Drizzle with 3 tbsp shoyu (soy sauce) and 1 tsp toasted sesame oil and fold gently.
3. Cover and refrigerate for 30 minutes (no more than 2 hours) so the flavors meld.
4. Divide 600 g warm cooked short-grain rice among bowls, top with the poke and 1 Persian cucumber, sliced, and sprinkle with 1 tbsp furikake.

Notes
Buy fish labeled sashimi or sushi grade from a counter you trust, and keep everything cold. Poke is best the day it's made. Inamona is roasted, crushed kukui nut with salt; macadamia is the usual substitute. For spicy ahi, swap the shoyu dressing for 3 tbsp Kewpie mixed with 1 tbsp sriracha and 1/2 tsp sesame oil.
