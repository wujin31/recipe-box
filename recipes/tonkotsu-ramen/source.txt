豚骨ラーメン (Tonkotsu Rāmen) · Hakata-Style Tonkotsu Ramen

Pork bones boiled hard for 12 hours into a milky, collagen-rich broth, seasoned with a kombu and bonito shio tare, with rolled chashu, marinated soft eggs and thin straight noodles. A weekend project. Serves 6.

Ingredients
For the broth:
• 1.5 kg pork trotters, split (ask the butcher)
• 1 kg pork neck bones or femur bones, cracked
• 6 L water, plus more to top up
• 1 onion, halved, skin on
• 1 head garlic, halved crosswise
• 50 g ginger, sliced
• 1 leek or 6 scallions, green parts
For the chashu and eggs:
• 1 kg skinless pork belly, in one slab
• 200 ml soy sauce
• 200 ml sake
• 100 ml mirin
• 50 g sugar
• 400 ml water (for the chashu)
• 20 g ginger, sliced (for the chashu)
• 6 eggs
For the shio tare:
• 250 ml water (for the tare)
• 10 g kombu
• 10 g katsuobushi (bonito flakes)
• 35 g salt
• 2 tbsp mirin (for the tare)
• 2 tbsp sake (for the tare)
To serve:
• 6 portions thin straight ramen noodles (about 720 g fresh)
• 20 g dried kikurage (wood ear), soaked and sliced
• 4 scallions, thinly sliced
• 4 tbsp beni shoga (red pickled ginger)
• 2 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
• 6 sheets nori, quartered

Steps
1. Put 1.5 kg pork trotters, split (ask the butcher) and 1 kg pork neck bones or femur bones, cracked in your largest stockpot, cover with cold water and bring to a boil. Boil 10 minutes, then drain. Scrub every piece under running water, removing dark clots and scum, and wash the pot.
2. Return the bones to the clean pot with 6 L water, plus more to top up. Bring to a hard, rolling boil and keep it there, lid ajar, for 12 hours. Top up with boiling water every 1 hour to keep the bones covered. The violent boil is what emulsifies the fat and collagen into a white broth; a simmer gives a clear one.
3. While the broth boils, make the chashu. Roll 1 kg skinless pork belly, in one slab up tightly lengthwise and tie it with butcher's twine every 2 cm. Sear it in a dry heavy pot over medium-high, turning, for 8 minutes until browned all over.
4. Add 200 ml soy sauce, 200 ml sake, 100 ml mirin, 50 g sugar, 400 ml water (for the chashu) and 20 g ginger, sliced (for the chashu). Bring to a boil, then cover with the lid ajar and simmer on low for 2 hours, turning the roll every 30 minutes. Let it cool in the liquid, then chill overnight so it slices cleanly.
5. Boil 6 eggs for 6 minutes 30 seconds, then plunge into ice water for 5 minutes and peel. Put them in a zip bag with 200 ml of the cooled chashu liquid (diluted with 100 ml water) and refrigerate at least 8 hours.
6. Make the tare: soak 10 g kombu in 250 ml water (for the tare) for 30 minutes, then heat until just simmering and remove the kombu. Add 10 g katsuobushi (bonito flakes), simmer 1 minute, and strain. Stir in 35 g salt, 2 tbsp mirin (for the tare) and 2 tbsp sake (for the tare) until dissolved.
7. For the broth's last 1 hour, add 1 onion, halved, skin on, 1 head garlic, halved crosswise, 50 g ginger, sliced and 1 leek or 6 scallions, green parts. Strain through a fine sieve. You want about 2.5 L of thick, opaque broth; boil it down further if thinner.
8. To serve, slice the chashu 5 mm thick and sear the slices in a hot pan 30 seconds per side. Halve the eggs. Warm 30 ml tare in each bowl and add 350 ml boiling broth, whisking.
9. Cook 6 portions thin straight ramen noodles (about 720 g fresh) in plenty of boiling unsalted water for 1 minute (Hakata noodles are served firm), drain hard, and add to the bowls.
10. Top with chashu, an egg, 20 g dried kikurage (wood ear), soaked and sliced, 4 scallions, thinly sliced, 4 tbsp beni shoga (red pickled ginger), 2 tbsp toasted sesame seeds and 6 sheets nori, quartered. Serve immediately.

Notes
Taste for salt by the bowl: tonkotsu broth itself is unseasoned, so the tare does all the work. Add more 5 ml at a time.

The broth, tare and chashu all freeze well; making them over two days is normal. A pressure cooker gets close in 3 hours on high (after blanching), then boil uncovered 30 minutes to emulsify. In Hakata, ask for 替え玉 (kaedama): a second portion of noodles dropped into the leftover broth.
