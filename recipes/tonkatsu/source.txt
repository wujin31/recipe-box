とんかつ (Tonkatsu) · Panko Pork Cutlet with Shredded Cabbage

Thick pork loin in a crisp, airy panko crust, fried and rested so it stays juicy, with a mound of ice-cold shredded cabbage, tonkatsu sauce, karashi and lemon. Serves 2.

Ingredients
• 2 boneless pork loin chops (about 350 g), 2 cm thick
• 1/2 tsp salt
• 1/4 tsp black pepper
• 30 g all-purpose flour
• 1 egg, beaten with 1 tsp oil
• 80 g panko (fresh panko if you can find it)
• 1 L neutral oil (for frying)
• 1/4 green cabbage (about 250 g)
• 4 tbsp tonkatsu sauce (Bull-Dog)
• 1 tbsp toasted white sesame seeds
• 2 tsp karashi (Japanese mustard)
• 1/2 lemon, cut into wedges
• 300 g hot cooked short-grain rice

Steps
1. Shred 1/4 green cabbage (about 250 g) as finely as you can (a mandoline helps) and soak in ice water for 5 minutes. Drain and spin very dry, then refrigerate.
2. Cut slits through the fat and silverskin along the edge of 2 boneless pork loin chops (about 350 g), 2 cm thick every 2 cm so it doesn't curl. Pound lightly to even out, reshape, and season with 1/2 tsp salt and 1/4 tsp black pepper.
3. Dredge in 30 g all-purpose flour, shaking off excess, dip into 1 egg, beaten with 1 tsp oil, then press gently into 80 g panko (fresh panko if you can find it) so the flakes stand up rather than flattening.
4. Heat 1 L neutral oil (for frying) to 170°C. Fry the chops for 3 minutes per side, until light golden. Lift onto a rack and rest 4 minutes; the carryover heat finishes the center.
5. Raise the oil to 180°C and fry again for 30 seconds per side, until deep golden and crisp.
6. Drain upright for 1 minute, then slice into 2 cm strips. Grind 1 tbsp toasted white sesame seeds and stir into 4 tbsp tonkatsu sauce (Bull-Dog) for dipping.
7. Serve with the cabbage, 2 tsp karashi (Japanese mustard), 1/2 lemon, cut into wedges and 300 g hot cooked short-grain rice.

Notes
The rest between fries keeps the pork pink-blushed and juicy while the crust gets a second crisp. Look for loin with a fat cap (rosu katsu); tenderloin (hire katsu) works but fry it 2 minutes per side.

Dress the cabbage with sesame dressing or more tonkatsu sauce. Leftover katsu becomes katsudon: simmer slices with onion in the oyakodon sauce and set with egg.
