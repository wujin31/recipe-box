Old-Fashioned Beef Stew

The Sunday classic: well-browned chuck braised low and slow in red wine and stock with tomato paste, thyme and bay, with carrots, potatoes and peas added late so they stay whole. Serves 6.

Ingredients
• 1.4 kg boneless beef chuck, cut into 4 cm cubes
• 2 tsp kosher salt
• 1 tsp black pepper
• 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
• 3 tbsp neutral oil
• 2 onions, chopped
• 2 celery stalks, chopped
• 4 garlic cloves, minced
• 2 tbsp tomato paste
• 250 ml dry red wine
• 1 L beef stock
• 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
• 4 sprigs fresh thyme
• 2 bay leaves
• 4 carrots, cut into 3 cm chunks
• 700 g Yukon Gold potatoes, cut into 4 cm chunks
• 150 g frozen peas
• 10 g parsley, chopped

Steps
1. Heat the oven to 160°C. Pat 1.4 kg boneless beef chuck, cut into 4 cm cubes dry, season with 2 tsp kosher salt and 1 tsp black pepper, and toss with 3 tbsp all-purpose flour.
2. Heat 1 tbsp of 3 tbsp neutral oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high. Brown the beef in three batches, 6 minutes each, until deeply browned on at least two sides, adding more oil as needed. Transfer to a bowl.
3. Lower the heat to medium. Add 2 onions, chopped and 2 celery stalks, chopped and cook 6 minutes, scraping up the browned bits. Add 4 garlic cloves, minced and 2 tbsp tomato paste and stir 2 minutes, until the paste darkens.
4. Pour in 250 ml dry red wine and simmer 3 minutes, scraping the bottom, until reduced by half. Add 1 L beef stock, 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce, 4 sprigs fresh thyme, 2 bay leaves and the beef with its juices. Bring to a simmer.
5. Cover and braise in the oven for 1 hour 30 minutes.
6. Stir in 4 carrots, cut into 3 cm chunks and 700 g Yukon Gold potatoes, cut into 4 cm chunks, cover and return to the oven for 1 hour, until the beef is fork-tender and the vegetables are cooked.
7. Remove the thyme stems and bay. Stir in 150 g frozen peas and simmer on the stove for 3 minutes. Season with salt and pepper and scatter with 10 g parsley, chopped.

Notes
Browning in batches is the most important step: crowded beef steams and the stew tastes flat. Adding the vegetables halfway keeps them from turning to mush. Like most braises, it's better the next day; skim the fat once chilled.
