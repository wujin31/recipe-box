Caramelized Shallot Pasta

Alison Roman's viral pantry pasta: a pile of thinly sliced shallots cooked slowly in olive oil until jammy, melted with anchovies, chili and a whole tin of tomato paste into a sticky, savory sauce for bucatini. Serves 4.

Ingredients
• 60 ml extra-virgin olive oil
• 6 large shallots (about 400 g), very thinly sliced
• 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
• 1 tsp salt
• 1/2 tsp black pepper
• 1 tsp red chili flakes
• 8 anchovy fillets (one 55 g tin), drained
• 170 g tomato paste (one small can)
• 280 g bucatini or spaghetti
• 1 tbsp salt (for the pasta water)
• 15 g parsley, finely chopped
• 1 tsp lemon zest
• 1 small garlic clove, finely chopped (for the topping)

Steps
1. Heat 60 ml extra-virgin olive oil in a large heavy pot over medium-high. Add 6 large shallots (about 400 g), very thinly sliced, 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced, 1 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp black pepper and cook 3 minutes, then lower to medium and cook 15 minutes, stirring often, until the shallots are completely soft, jammy and golden brown at the edges.
2. Add 1 tsp red chili flakes and 8 anchovy fillets (one 55 g tin), drained and stir 2 minutes, until the anchovies melt into the oil.
3. Add 170 g tomato paste (one small can) and cook 5 minutes, stirring constantly, until it turns from bright red to a deep rust color and starts to caramelize on the bottom of the pot. Take off the heat; set aside about a third of the mixture (it keeps for later).
4. Cook 280 g bucatini or spaghetti in boiling water with 1 tbsp salt (for the pasta water) until al dente. Reserve 360 ml pasta water.
5. Add the pasta and 240 ml pasta water to the pot with the shallot sauce. Toss over medium heat for 2 minutes, adding more water as needed, until the pasta is coated and glossy.
6. Mix 15 g parsley, finely chopped, 1 tsp lemon zest and 1 small garlic clove, finely chopped (for the topping) and scatter over the pasta to serve.

Notes
Adapted from Alison Roman's NYT Cooking recipe. Don't rush the shallots; the long cook is where the sweetness comes from. The reserved third of the shallot mixture keeps 2 weeks in the fridge: spread it on toast, stir it into eggs, or make the next batch of pasta.

No cheese needed; the anchovies supply the savoriness without tasting fishy.
