Spaghetti alla Carbonara — Guanciale, Egg and Pecorino

The Roman original with five ingredients and no cream: crisp guanciale, a thick paste of yolks, Pecorino Romano and black pepper, and pasta water, tossed off the heat into a glossy, silky sauce. Serves 4.

Ingredients
• 400 g spaghetti or rigatoni
• 1 tbsp salt (for the pasta water)
• 150 g guanciale, rind removed, cut into 1 cm strips
• 4 egg yolks
• 2 whole eggs
• 80 g Pecorino Romano, finely grated, plus more to serve
• 2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

Steps
1. Put 150 g guanciale, rind removed, cut into 1 cm strips in a cold large skillet and set over medium-low. Cook 10 minutes, stirring, until the fat has rendered and the strips are crisp outside but still a little tender. Turn off the heat.
2. Whisk 4 egg yolks, 2 whole eggs, 80 g Pecorino Romano, finely grated, plus more to serve and most of 2 tsp freshly ground black pepper into a thick paste.
3. Bring 3 L water to a boil with 1 tbsp salt (for the pasta water) (use less salt than usual; the cheese and guanciale are salty). Cook 400 g spaghetti or rigatoni 1 minute less than the package says.
4. Transfer the pasta to the skillet with the guanciale and its fat, add a splash of pasta water, and toss over low heat for 1 minute to coat. Take the pan off the heat and wait 30 seconds.
5. Whisk 2 tbsp of hot pasta water into the egg paste, then pour it over the pasta and toss vigorously for 1 minute, adding pasta water a tablespoon at a time, until the sauce is creamy and glossy, not scrambled.
6. Serve immediately with the rest of the pepper and more Pecorino.

Notes
The eggs set at about 70°C. The pan should be warm, not hot: off the heat after the 30-second wait. If the sauce stays runny, set the pan over the pasta pot for a few seconds while tossing (a bain-marie). Pancetta works if you can't find guanciale; bacon is smoky and not traditional.
