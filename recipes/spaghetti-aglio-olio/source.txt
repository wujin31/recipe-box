Spaghetti Aglio, Olio e Peperoncino — Garlic, Oil and Chili Pasta

The Roman late-night pantry pasta: garlic gently sizzled in plenty of olive oil with dried chili, emulsified with starchy pasta water into a light, clinging sauce. Serves 2.

Ingredients
• 200 g spaghetti
• 1 tbsp salt (for the pasta water)
• 60 ml extra-virgin olive oil
• 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
• 1 dried peperoncino, crumbled (or 1/2 tsp red chili flakes)
• 10 g flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
• 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil (to finish)

Steps
1. Bring 2 L water to a boil with 1 tbsp salt (for the pasta water). Add 200 g spaghetti and cook 2 minutes less than the package time.
2. Meanwhile, put 60 ml extra-virgin olive oil and 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced in a large cold skillet and set over low heat. Cook 4 minutes, swirling, until the garlic is pale golden at the edges; don't let it brown. Add 1 dried peperoncino, crumbled (or 1/2 tsp red chili flakes) for the last 30 seconds.
3. Add a ladle of pasta water to the skillet; it will sputter and turn cloudy.
4. Transfer the pasta to the skillet with tongs and toss over medium-high heat for 2 minutes, adding splashes of pasta water, until the pasta is al dente and the sauce is creamy and clings.
5. Off the heat, toss in 10 g flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped and drizzle with 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil (to finish). Serve immediately.

Notes
Start the garlic in cold oil so it infuses before it colors; browned garlic turns bitter and is the most common mistake. The emulsion is the dish: keep tossing and adding water until the oil and water turn into a glossy coating rather than a puddle.

No cheese in the Roman original. Toasted breadcrumbs (pangrattato) are the southern Italian "poor man's parmesan" if you want crunch.
