麻婆豆腐 (Mápó Dòufu) · Mapo Tofu with Ground Pork

The Sichuan classic: soft tofu simmered in a fiery, glossy sauce of Pixian doubanjiang, fermented black beans and chili with crisp ground pork, finished with a numbing dust of roasted Sichuan pepper. Serves 3.

Ingredients
• 450 g soft or medium tofu, cut into 2 cm cubes
• 1 tsp salt (for blanching)
• 2 tbsp neutral oil
• 150 g ground pork
• 2 tbsp Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣)
• 1 tbsp fermented black beans (douchi), rinsed and chopped
• 1 tbsp Sichuan chili flakes or ground chili
• 3 garlic cloves, minced
• 10 g ginger, minced
• 300 ml chicken stock
• 1 tsp soy sauce
• 1/2 tsp sugar
• 2 tsp cornstarch mixed with 3 tbsp water
• 3 scallions or garlic sprouts, cut into 2 cm lengths
• 1 tsp roasted ground Sichuan peppercorns
• 600 g hot cooked rice

Steps
1. Bring a pot of water to a simmer with 1 tsp salt (for blanching). Slide in 450 g soft or medium tofu, cut into 2 cm cubes and simmer gently for 2 minutes, then leave it in the hot water while you start the sauce.
2. Heat 2 tbsp neutral oil in a wok over medium-high. Fry 150 g ground pork for 4 minutes, breaking it up, until browned and crisp.
3. Lower the heat to medium-low, push the pork aside and fry 2 tbsp Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣) in the oil for 1 minute, until the oil turns red. Add 1 tbsp fermented black beans (douchi), rinsed and chopped, 1 tbsp Sichuan chili flakes or ground chili, 3 garlic cloves, minced and 10 g ginger, minced and stir 30 seconds.
4. Add 300 ml chicken stock, 1 tsp soy sauce and 1/2 tsp sugar and bring to a boil. Drain the tofu and slide it in. Simmer 5 minutes, gently pushing (not stirring) with the back of a spatula so the tofu doesn't break.
5. Stir 2 tsp cornstarch mixed with 3 tbsp water and add it in three additions, 30 seconds apart, nudging the tofu, until the sauce clings and the oil pools red at the edges. Add 3 scallions or garlic sprouts, cut into 2 cm lengths for the last 30 seconds.
6. Transfer to a bowl, sprinkle with 1 tsp roasted ground Sichuan peppercorns and serve with 600 g hot cooked rice.

Notes
Blanching the tofu in salted water seasons it and firms the surface so the cubes survive the wok. Pixian doubanjiang is the irreplaceable ingredient; fry it slowly so it reddens the oil without scorching.

Beef is the traditional Chengdu meat; pork is the common home version. Roast whole Sichuan peppercorns in a dry pan for 2 minutes, then grind; pre-ground loses its tingle quickly.
