Arroz Rojo, Short-Grain Adaptation — Zojirushi Neuro Fuzzy

Mexican red rice adapted for Japanese short-grain. Rice is rinsed hard and toasted in oil (pilaf-style) to fight short-grain's natural stickiness, then finished in the Neuro Fuzzy on Mixed. Expect a softer, slightly clingy grain — a Mexican-Japanese hybrid, not taquería-fluffy.

Ingredients
• 2 cup Japanese short-grain rice
• 3 tbsp neutral oil
• 1/2 medium white onion, finely diced
• 3 garlic cloves, minced
• 3/4 cup tomato purée / passata
• 1 1/2 cup chicken broth
• 2 tsp tomato bouillon (Knorr Caldo de Tomate)
• 1/2 tsp ground cumin
• 1 tsp fine salt
• 1/2 cup frozen peas & diced carrots (optional)
• 2 tbsp cilantro, chopped
• 1 lime, wedged

Steps
1. Rinse 2 cup Japanese short-grain rice thoroughly, swirling and changing the water 3–4 times until it runs mostly clear — short-grain sheds a lot of surface starch and you want it gone. Drain very well and let sit to dry. Do NOT soak.
2. Heat 3 tbsp neutral oil in your stainless skillet over medium-high. Add 2 cup Japanese short-grain rice and stir constantly until the grains look chalky-white turning faintly golden and smell nutty, about 7 min. This oil coat is your main anti-clump insurance — don't shortcut it. Keep it moving so the starch doesn't scorch on stainless.
3. Add 1/2 medium white onion, finely diced and cook until soft and translucent, ~3 min. Add 3 garlic cloves, minced and stir 30 seconds until fragrant.
4. Stir in 3/4 cup tomato purée / passata, 2 tsp tomato bouillon (Knorr Caldo de Tomate), and 1/2 tsp ground cumin. Cook 2 min until it darkens slightly and goes paste-like, concentrating flavor before liquid.
5. Scrape everything into the Neuro Fuzzy inner pan. Add 1 1/2 cup chicken broth, 1 tsp fine salt, and 1/2 cup frozen peas & diced carrots (optional). Stir once to combine and level the rice under the liquid — then leave it alone.
6. Select MIXED (the takikomi-style program — handles the oil and add-ins). If unavailable, use WHITE/SUSHI. Never Quick. Start and keep the lid shut the whole cycle.
7. When the cycle ends, leave it on Keep Warm undisturbed for 10 min. Then fluff GENTLY with the paddle — cut and lift, don't stir — fold in 2 tbsp cilantro, chopped, and serve with 1 lime, wedged.

Notes
⚠️ UNTESTED — needs a real cook before it goes in the compendium.

Texture reality: short-grain will read softer and more cohesive than long-grain arroz rojo. The toast + oil coat is what keeps it from going full sticky-rice. If you want to push it firmer/more separate next time, the levers are (a) toast a minute longer and (b) shave another 2 tbsp off the broth.

Liquid ratio: 2.25 cups total (0.75 purée + 1.5 broth) per 2 cups rice = ~1.125:1. Deliberately lean — short-grain absorbs less than long-grain AND the sealed cooker barely evaporates. Two ways to overshoot into mush here, so I cut aggressively.

Do NOT soak: normal Japanese rice prep soaks, but soaked grains steam instead of toast. Rinse well, drain, toast dry. Skip the soak entirely for this dish.

Mixed setting is ideal here — it's essentially the takikomi-gohan program (short-grain + add-ins + seasoning), so it's native to what you're doing. Use it if you have it.

Secret weapon: tomato bouillon (Caldo de Tomate) carries the color and savory depth. Don't skip it.

Fluff gently: short-grain sheds starch and turns gluey when agitated hot. Minimal folding at the end.
