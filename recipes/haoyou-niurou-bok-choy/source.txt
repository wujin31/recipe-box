蠔油牛肉炒白菜 (Háoyóu Niúròu Chǎo Báicài) · Stir-Fried Beef with Oyster Sauce and Bok Choy

The everyday Cantonese stir-fry: velveted flank steak seared fast over high heat, tossed with crisp-tender bok choy in a glossy oyster sauce gravy. 20 minutes. Serves 3.

Ingredients
For the beef:
• 340 g flank steak, sliced against the grain 5 mm thick
• 1/4 tsp baking soda
• 1 tbsp water (for the marinade)
• 1 tsp soy sauce (for the marinade)
• 1 tsp Shaoxing wine
• 2 tsp cornstarch (for the marinade)
• 1 tsp neutral oil (for the marinade)
For the sauce:
• 2 tbsp oyster sauce
• 1 tsp soy sauce
• 1/2 tsp dark soy sauce
• 1 tsp sugar
• 1/2 tsp sesame oil
• 80 ml chicken stock or water
• 1 tsp cornstarch
For the stir-fry:
• 2 tbsp neutral oil
• 450 g baby bok choy, halved or quartered
• 3 garlic cloves, sliced
• 4 slices ginger
• 600 g hot cooked rice

Steps
1. Toss 340 g flank steak, sliced against the grain 5 mm thick with 1/4 tsp baking soda and 1 tbsp water (for the marinade) until absorbed, then with 1 tsp soy sauce (for the marinade), 1 tsp Shaoxing wine and 2 tsp cornstarch (for the marinade). Stir in 1 tsp neutral oil (for the marinade) and marinate 20 minutes.
2. Whisk 2 tbsp oyster sauce, 1 tsp soy sauce, 1/2 tsp dark soy sauce, 1 tsp sugar, 1/2 tsp sesame oil, 80 ml chicken stock or water and 1 tsp cornstarch.
3. Heat a wok over high heat until smoking. Add 1 tbsp of 2 tbsp neutral oil, spread the beef in one layer and sear 1 minute without moving, then toss 30 seconds, until just browned but still pink in places. Transfer to a plate.
4. Add the rest of the oil, 4 slices ginger and 3 garlic cloves, sliced and stir 15 seconds. Add 450 g baby bok choy, halved or quartered and stir-fry 1 minute, then add 2 tbsp water and cover for 1 minute, until the stems are crisp-tender.
5. Return the beef, stir the sauce and pour it in, and toss 30 seconds, until thick and glossy. Serve with 600 g hot cooked rice.

Notes
Velveting with baking soda and cornstarch keeps flank steak tender even when it's cooked through. The wok must be very hot and the beef in a single layer; crowding makes it steam and turn grey. Gai lan or choy sum are classic swaps for the bok choy.
