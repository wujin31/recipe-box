紅燒牛肉麵 (Hóngshāo Niúròu Miàn) · Taiwanese Red-Braised Beef Noodle Soup

Taiwan's national dish: beef shank and tendon braised with spicy doubanjiang, tomato, soy and warm spices until tender, served over chewy wheat noodles with bok choy and pickled mustard greens. Serves 6.

Ingredients
For the braise:
• 1.2 kg boneless beef shank, cut into 4 cm chunks
• 500 g beef tendon, left whole (optional)
• 2 tbsp neutral oil
• 40 g ginger, sliced
• 10 garlic cloves, smashed
• 6 scallions, cut into 5 cm lengths
• 1 onion, cut into wedges
• 3 tbsp spicy doubanjiang (辣豆瓣醬)
• 2 tomatoes, cut into wedges
• 20 g rock sugar or 1 1/2 tbsp sugar
• 80 ml Shaoxing wine or Taiwanese rice wine (米酒)
• 80 ml soy sauce
• 2 tbsp dark soy sauce
• 2.5 L water
• 1 tsp salt, to taste
For the spice bag:
• 3 star anise
• 1 cinnamon stick
• 3 bay leaves
• 1 tsp Sichuan peppercorns
• 1 tsp fennel seeds
• 3 dried red chilies
For the pickled mustard greens:
• 150 g pickled mustard greens (酸菜), rinsed and chopped
• 1 tbsp neutral oil (for the greens)
• 2 tsp sugar (for the greens)
To serve:
• 750 g fresh wide wheat noodles
• 300 g baby bok choy, halved
• 3 scallions, finely chopped
• 1 small bunch cilantro, chopped

Steps
1. Put 1.2 kg boneless beef shank, cut into 4 cm chunks and 500 g beef tendon, left whole (optional) in a large pot, cover with cold water and bring to a boil. Boil 5 minutes, then drain and rinse off the scum.
2. Tie 3 star anise, 1 cinnamon stick, 3 bay leaves, 1 tsp Sichuan peppercorns, 1 tsp fennel seeds and 3 dried red chilies in a spice bag or cheesecloth.
3. Heat 2 tbsp neutral oil in a large heavy pot over medium. Fry 40 g ginger, sliced, 10 garlic cloves, smashed and 6 scallions, cut into 5 cm lengths for 2 minutes until fragrant, add 1 onion, cut into wedges and cook 3 minutes. Add 3 tbsp spicy doubanjiang (辣豆瓣醬) and fry 1 minute, until the oil turns red. Add 2 tomatoes, cut into wedges and 20 g rock sugar or 1 1/2 tbsp sugar and cook 3 minutes, until the tomatoes start to break down.
4. Add the blanched beef and tendon and stir to coat. Pour in 80 ml Shaoxing wine or Taiwanese rice wine (米酒), 80 ml soy sauce and 2 tbsp dark soy sauce, and let it bubble 1 minute. Add 2.5 L water and the spice bag, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer on low for 2 hours, until the shank is tender. If using tendon, take out the shank and keep simmering the tendon for 1 hour more (or until a chopstick slides through), then return the shank.
5. Discard the spice bag, the scallion and as much of the ginger as you can find. Skim the fat, slice the tendon into pieces, and season the broth with 1 tsp salt, to taste.
6. Meanwhile, stir-fry 150 g pickled mustard greens (酸菜), rinsed and chopped in 1 tbsp neutral oil (for the greens) over high heat for 2 minutes with 2 tsp sugar (for the greens) until dry and fragrant.
7. Boil 750 g fresh wide wheat noodles in a separate pot of unsalted water for 3 minutes (or per the package), adding 300 g baby bok choy, halved for the last 1 minute. Drain.
8. Divide the noodles and bok choy among large bowls, ladle over the hot broth with beef and tendon, and top with 3 scallions, finely chopped, 1 small bunch cilantro, chopped and the pickled mustard greens.

Notes
The doubanjiang is what makes it taste Taiwanese rather than generic Chinese braised beef: fry it in oil until red before any liquid goes in. Shank gives gelatinous, sliceable meat; chuck works but falls apart.

It's better the next day. Chill the broth and lift off the fat cap. Pressure cooker: after step 4's liquids, cook on high 45 minutes (tendon 70 minutes), then release naturally. Chili oil and a splash of black vinegar at the table are common.
