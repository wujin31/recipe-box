Aguachile Verde — Sinaloa-Style Shrimp in Green Chile-Lime

Butterflied raw shrimp "cooked" briefly in a blended lime, serrano and cilantro water, piled with cucumber and red onion and eaten right away on tostadas. Serves 4 as a starter.

Ingredients
• 450 g large raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
• 1 tsp salt
• 180 ml fresh lime juice (about 8 limes)
• 3 serrano chiles, stemmed
• 20 g cilantro, with stems
• 1 small garlic clove
• 60 ml cold water
• 1 cucumber, peeled, seeded and sliced into half-moons
• 1/2 red onion, very thinly sliced
• 1 avocado, sliced
• 8 tostadas or saltine crackers

Steps
1. Butterfly 450 g large raw shrimp, peeled and deveined: cut almost all the way through along the back and open them flat. Put them on a chilled platter in a single layer and sprinkle with 1 tsp salt.
2. Blend 180 ml fresh lime juice (about 8 limes), 3 serrano chiles, stemmed, 20 g cilantro, with stems, 1 small garlic clove and 60 ml cold water for 30 seconds, until bright green and smooth.
3. Pour the chile-lime water over the shrimp and scatter 1/2 red onion, very thinly sliced and 1 cucumber, peeled, seeded and sliced into half-moons on top. Refrigerate 15 minutes, until the shrimp turn just pink-opaque at the edges but stay translucent in the center.
4. Top with 1 avocado, sliced and serve immediately with 8 tostadas or saltine crackers.

Notes
Unlike ceviche, aguachile isn't left to marinate: in Sinaloa it's served within minutes, so the shrimp stays tender and sweet. Use the freshest shrimp you can find (frozen-at-sea shrimp, thawed in the fridge, are often fresher than "fresh"). A dash of Maggi or Clamato in the blender is a popular local touch.
