焼きそば (Yakisoba) · Japanese Stir-Fried Noodles

Festival-stall yakisoba: steamed wheat noodles fried hard on a hot pan with pork belly and cabbage, tossed in a tangy homemade Worcestershire-based sauce and finished with aonori, katsuobushi and red ginger. Serves 3.

Ingredients
For the sauce:
• 3 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
• 1 tbsp oyster sauce
• 1 tbsp ketchup
• 2 tsp soy sauce
• 2 tsp sugar
For the noodles:
• 3 packs steamed yakisoba noodles (450 g)
• 2 tbsp neutral oil
• 150 g thinly sliced pork belly, cut into 4 cm pieces
• 1/2 onion, sliced
• 1/2 carrot, cut into thin strips
• 200 g green cabbage, cut into 3 cm pieces
• 100 g bean sprouts
• 1/4 tsp salt
• 1/4 tsp black pepper
To serve:
• 2 tsp aonori (seaweed flakes)
• 5 g katsuobushi (bonito flakes)
• 2 tbsp beni shoga (red pickled ginger)

Steps
1. Mix 3 tbsp Worcestershire sauce, 1 tbsp oyster sauce, 1 tbsp ketchup, 2 tsp soy sauce and 2 tsp sugar.
2. Microwave 3 packs steamed yakisoba noodles (450 g) in their bags for 1 minute, then loosen them with your fingers.
3. Heat 1 tbsp of 2 tbsp neutral oil in a large skillet over medium-high. Spread the noodles out and fry undisturbed for 2 minutes per side, until spotted brown. Transfer to a plate.
4. Add the rest of the oil and 150 g thinly sliced pork belly, cut into 4 cm pieces. Fry 2 minutes, until the fat renders and the edges crisp. Season with 1/4 tsp salt and 1/4 tsp black pepper.
5. Add 1/2 onion, sliced and 1/2 carrot, cut into thin strips and stir-fry 1 minute, then 200 g green cabbage, cut into 3 cm pieces and stir-fry 2 minutes, until just wilted. Add 100 g bean sprouts.
6. Return the noodles, pour in the sauce and toss over high heat for 1 minute, until the sauce is absorbed and starts to caramelize on the pan.
7. Plate and top with 2 tsp aonori (seaweed flakes), 5 g katsuobushi (bonito flakes) and 2 tbsp beni shoga (red pickled ginger).

Notes
Frying the noodles alone first gives them toasted, chewy edges, the stall flavor. Otafuku or Bull-Dog yakisoba sauce in place of the homemade one is just as authentic: use 5 tbsp. A fried egg on top makes it a meal.
