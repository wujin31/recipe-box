銀ダラの西京焼き (Gindara no Saikyō-yaki) · Miso-Glazed Black Cod

Nobu's famous dish, from the old Kyoto tradition of saikyo-yaki: black cod cured for 2 to 3 days in sweet white miso, sake and mirin, then broiled until caramelized and finished in the oven. Serves 4.

Ingredients
• 4 black cod (sablefish) fillets, about 170 g each, skin on
• 60 ml sake
• 60 ml mirin
• 180 g white miso (saikyo miso if possible)
• 90 g sugar
• 1/2 tsp salt
• 4 hajikami (pickled ginger shoots) or pickled ginger, to serve

Steps
1. Bring 60 ml sake and 60 ml mirin to a boil in a saucepan and boil 20 seconds to cook off the alcohol. Turn the heat to low and whisk in 180 g white miso (saikyo miso if possible) until smooth. Raise the heat, add 90 g sugar and stir until it dissolves, about 1 minute. Cool completely.
2. Sprinkle 4 black cod (sablefish) fillets, about 170 g each, skin on lightly with 1/2 tsp salt, leave 20 minutes, then pat dry.
3. Spread a third of the marinade in a dish, lay in the fish and cover with the rest. Cover tightly and refrigerate 2 to 3 days.
4. Heat the oven to 200°C. Wipe the excess marinade off the fish with your fingers and paper towels; don't rinse it.
5. Broil the fillets skin side down on an oiled rack 15 cm from the heat for 4 minutes, until the top is browned and blackened in spots.
6. Move to the middle of the oven and bake 10 minutes, until the fish flakes and is just opaque.
7. Serve with 4 hajikami (pickled ginger shoots) or pickled ginger, to serve.

Notes
The salting step and 2 to 3 day cure season the fish all the way through and firm its texture. One day works but is milder. Watch the broiler closely: the sugar in the miso goes from caramelized to burnt fast.

Black cod (sablefish) is rich enough to stay silky; salmon, Chilean sea bass or mackerel work too, with 1 day of cure. Leftover marinade keeps a week for a second batch.
